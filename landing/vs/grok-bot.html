bleep vs Grok Bot

Grok Bot gives you cloud teammates that do work in your apps. bleep is a free, open source buddy on your Mac that watches your day and your coding agents. Here is how they compare, and when to pick which.

Updated 18 Nov 2026 · by Aviroop Paul, who makes bleep · bleep 0.5.0

Short answer

  • Pick Grok Bot if you want cloud agents that do long, multi-step work inside other apps and websites for you, and you already pay for SuperGrok Heavy or Cursor Ultra.
  • Pick bleep if you want a free assistant on your Mac that watches your mail, calendar and coding agents, speaks first when something needs you, and keeps your data on your machine.

What Grok Bot does better

  • It does the work itself. Each bot has its own computer with a browser and a terminal. It can operate apps and websites that have no API.
  • Many bots at once. You can run several bots in parallel, each with its own memory, like a small team.
  • Routines from demos. Show a bot how you do a task once and it can repeat it.
  • On your phone. It has an iOS app. bleep is Mac only.

What bleep does better

  • It is free and you can start today. No paid plan, no waitlist.
  • Your data stays on your Mac. bleep has no server. Grok Bot signs in to your accounts from xAI's cloud.
  • It speaks first. bleep is built to notice things: a meeting in an hour, a bill due tonight, a reply that waits on you.
  • It keeps your coding agents moving. bleep sees every Claude Code and Codex session that waits on you and approves the safe asks. Rules decide, not a model, so a bad guess cannot approve a git push --force.
  • You pick the model. Use the Claude plan you already pay for, any API key, or a local model with Ollama.
  • Open source. Read the code, change it, or audit what it does with your mail.

Can you use both?

Yes. They do different jobs. Grok Bot is a worker you hand tasks to. bleep is a buddy that watches your day and tells you what needs you. Many people want the second one first: fewer missed meetings and fewer agents stuck on a yes.

What bleep is

  • Lives on your Mac. A native app with a small Rust core. A floating buddy on every Space. Double-tap ⌥ to call it.
  • Speaks first. It checks your mail, calendar and coding agents on its own, and taps you before a meeting, when a bill is due, when a mail waits on you, or when an agent is stuck.
  • Unblocks your coding agents. It approves the safe Claude Code and Codex asks your rules allow, like tests, lint and file reads. Push, deploy, delete, secrets and money always wait for you.
  • Any model. Your Claude Code plan by default, an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter or Groq, or a local model in Ollama or LM Studio.
  • Private. No bleep server, no account, no telemetry. Mail, memory and logs stay in ~/.bleep.
  • Free and open source. MIT license, source on GitHub.

Questions

Is there a free Grok Bot alternative for Mac?

Yes. bleep is free and open source, and runs on your Mac. It is not a clone: it does not run cloud computers or click through apps. It watches your mail, calendar and coding agents, speaks first when something needs you, and does small jobs after your yes.

Is there an open source Grok Bot alternative?

Several. OpenMausBot and rakazo copy Grok Bot's team-of-bots design. bleep is open source too (MIT), but it is a single proactive assistant on your Mac rather than a bot team. See all alternatives.

Does bleep need a Grok or xAI account?

No. bleep uses your Claude Code plan by default. You can also use an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter or Groq, or a local model.

Can bleep operate apps for me like Grok Bot?

Not in the same way. bleep works through connectors: Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, files on your Mac, and any remote MCP server. It does not control a browser or a virtual machine.
